# Every Cell Gets a Receipt: Research Brief on Cell-Level Citations in Fill with Gemini

Google did add per-cell web-source citations to Fill with Gemini in Google Sheets, but the official announcement is dated 28 October 2026, not 30 October, and Google's own help documentation suggests a per-cell "Sources" view existed earlier for Experiments/Beta users, so the hook's date and its "none showed where it came from" framing need correcting; the 41% sampling figure is mathematically correct for the binomial model (and becomes about 45% for a 20-row column under the exact hypergeometric model).

**TL;DR**
- The feature is real and documented: Google's Workspace Updates post of 28 October 2026 says that when the AI function generates cell values, "Gemini will now provide the exact web sources referenced directly within each individual cell," on Sheets for the web, with rollout from 20 October 2026; 30 October is only the date of the weekly recap that relisted it.
- The module's core statistical claim holds: with 1 error in 10 cells, a random 5-cell check catches at least one error with probability 1 − 0.9⁵ = 40.95% (binomial); for a 20-row column containing exactly 2 wrong cells, the exact (hypergeometric) figure is 44.7%, and for the 40-cell headquarters-plus-headcount sheet with 4 wrong cells it is 42.7%.
- No retrieved source reports a hands-on audit of the new Sources panel (as of 25 November 2026); the "three failure modes" are supported by general citation research and tests of other Gemini surfaces, and the "four-step trust ladder" is the module's own construct, not a documented industry framework.

## 1. What it is and what changed

**The development.** On 28 October 2026, Google's Workspace team published "Fill with Gemini now provides cell-level citations for web sources and supports larger requests" on the official Workspace Updates blog [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25].\[1\]\[2\] The post states: "When you use the AI function to generate cell values, Gemini will now provide the exact web sources referenced directly within each individual cell. This cell-level source attribution gives you full visibility to audit, verify, and cross-reference outputs for greater trust and accuracy. Sources are attributed to each individual cell and are available in Google Sheets on the web." [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25]\[1\]

**Dates.** The post is dated October 28, 2026. Its rollout section says "Gradual rollout (up to 15 days for feature visibility) started on October 20, 2026" for Rapid Release and Scheduled Release domains [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25]. The "Google Workspace Weekly Recap - October 30, 2026" relisted the item with a "Learn more" link back to the 28 October post [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/weekly-recap-10-30-2026.html?m=1, accessed 2026-11-25].\[3\]\[4\] So 30 October is a recap date, not the launch date.

**Who it is for.** The post lists availability for Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, consumer Google AI Pro and Ultra, Google AI Pro for Education, and the AI Expanded Access add-on. It is "available by default to users with Gemini in Sheets enabled" [MONITOR] [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25].

**What existed before.**
- Google introduced the AI function (`=AI()` / `=Gemini()`) to Sheets in a Workspace Updates post titled "Generate data with Gemini in Google Sheets," dated 25 June 2025. - On 22 April 2026, Google introduced Fill with Gemini, describing it as "an entry point to the existing AI function." It has two triggers: drag-and-drop from a column with at least one completed cell, and prompt-based "Fill" on empty multi-cell selections [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/effortlessly-automate-data-entry-in-Google-Sheets-using-Fill-with-Gemini.html, accessed 2026-11-25]. That April post claimed users could "populate data 9x faster than manual entry for 100-cell tasks" based on "a 95-participant study," and it makes no mention of sources or citations [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/effortlessly-automate-data-entry-in-Google-Sheets-using-Fill-with-Gemini.html, accessed 2026-11-25].

**Why it matters: technical reader.** Each citation is attached to a single cell, the same unit as the output. That means provenance can be audited row by row, instead of matching one list of links against dozens of values. Google's help page describes the steps: "To view the web sources used for a response, click into a cell, hover over the content and then click Sources" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25]. Saurabh Shukla of the EchoNerve newsletter states the engineering limit on 4 November 2026: "A source in a cell shows where the AI looked. It does not show that the page says what the cell says." [source: https://echonerve.substack.com/p/how-to-check-3000-ai-filled-spreadsheet, accessed 2026-11-25]

**Why it matters: non-technical reader.** Google itself presents the change as an audit aid. The announcement's image caption reads "Easily audit and verify your Gemini-generated data in Google Sheets using the new cell-level web source citations." [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25]. For a manager, the practical change is that checking now has a defined unit of work: open one cell's sources. That makes it possible to budget checks and sample them. It does not remove the need to check, because Google's help page also warns "Gemini features may suggest inaccurate or inappropriate information" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].

## 2. How it works

The steps below run from the user's action to the auditor's decision. Google documents steps 1–4 and 6. Step 5 is documented for Google's developer API but not for Sheets, and is labelled as such.

**Step 1: A user asks Sheets to fill empty cells.** Fill with Gemini starts in one of two ways: dragging down from a column that has at least one completed cell, or selecting empty cells and clicking "Fill," with an optional custom prompt [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].

**Step 2: That action runs the AI function underneath.** Google's April 2026 post says Fill with Gemini "is an entry point to the existing AI function" [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/effortlessly-automate-data-entry-in-Google-Sheets-using-Fill-with-Gemini.html, accessed 2026-11-25]. The AI function's syntax is `AI("prompt", [optional range])`. It "doesn't have access to your entire spreadsheet," so the range argument is what gives each row its context, and Google calls it "highly recommended to improve output quality" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25]. In a company list, each headquarters or headcount cell is built from the company name in column A.

**Step 3: For factual questions, the function consults Google Search.** Since October 2025 the AI function is "automatically grounding it in results from Google Search" [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/10/enhanced-ai-function-sheets-google-search.html, accessed 2026-11-25].\[6\] Google's help page lists "Access real-time information from Google Search" among the function's uses and gives examples such as `=AI("What is the population of the list capital city?", A2:B2)` [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].\[8\]

**Step 4: The model writes a value, and the pages it used are attached to that cell.** According to the 28 October post, Gemini provides "the exact web sources referenced directly within each individual cell" [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25]. The reader opens them by clicking into the cell, hovering, and clicking **Sources** [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].\[8\]\[9\]

**Step 5: How a grounded answer is linked to its sources (an analogy; not documented for Sheets).** Google does not publish how Sheets does this internally. The closest public analogue is Google's developer documentation for "Grounding with Google Search." There, a grounded response carries `groundingMetadata`, including `groundingChunks` (the sources) and `groundingSupports`, described as an "Array of chunks to connect model response text to the sources in groundingChunks" [source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/google-search, accessed 2026-11-25]. Google Cloud's reference describes groundingSupports as "A list of grounding supports that connect the generated content to the grounding chunks" [source: https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/reference/rest/v1beta1/GroundingMetadata, accessed 2026-11-25].\[10\] If Sheets works the same way, the consequence for auditors is that the link records that a source was used for a piece of text, not that the source states the value. Treat this as an engineering analogy until Google documents how Sheets works internally.

**Step 6: The value is frozen into the cell and does not update itself.** When the user clicks "Generate and Insert," "generated content is inserted and the cell edit is attributed to you in version history" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].\[8\] Google also notes that when prompts are built by joining cells together, "if there's a change in the data in ranges that are part of the prompt, you don't get a prompt to refresh the data" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25]. The effect: the value is a snapshot. If a cited web page changes after generation, a once-correct cell can become out of date, and its citation then points to the page as it is now, not as it was.

**Step 7: Why citations create a sampling problem, not a solution.** A citation makes one cell cheaper to check, but a single request can now cover "up to 3,000 cells" [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25]. Shukla states the resulting problem directly: "Nobody opens 3,000 sources. That is the problem. A citation per cell makes a bulk fill feel verified, and the feeling is cheap to get." [source: https://echonerve.substack.com/p/how-to-check-3000-ai-filled-spreadsheet, accessed 2026-11-25]\[2\]

**Step 8: The sampling arithmetic behind the module's 41%.**
- Model: each checked cell is either wrong or right, with the same error probability p for every cell. The NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook defines the binomial distribution for "exactly two mutually exclusive outcomes of a trial" and notes it "assumes that p is fixed for all trials." Its probability mass function is P(x; p, n) = C(n, x) pˣ(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ˣ [source: https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda366i.htm, accessed 2026-11-25].
- When p = 0.1, the chance that a 5-cell sample contains no wrong cells is P(0; 0.1, 5) = 0.9⁵ = 0.59049. The chance of catching at least one error is 1 − 0.59049 = 0.40951, i.e. 41%. (Computed from the NIST formula.)
- Correction for a short list: a real audit samples without replacement. NIST's glossary states that "Unlike the binomial distribution, the hypergeometric distribution assumes sampling is without replacement" [source: https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/glossary.htm, accessed 2026-11-25]. Researchers studying annotation inspection report that the binomial approximation "can lead to inaccuracies" and that "care needs to be taken to only approximate if the dataset size is at least 10 times larger than the sample size" [source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11919, accessed 2026-11-25].
  - 20-row column, exactly 2 wrong (1 in 10), sample of 5: P(no error) = C(18,5)/C(20,5) = 8,568/15,504 = 0.5526, so the catch probability is 44.7%. (Computed.)
  - 40-cell sheet (20 companies × headquarters + headcount), exactly 4 wrong, sample of 5: P(no error) = C(36,5)/C(40,5) = 376,992/658,008 = 0.5729, so the catch probability is 42.7%. (Computed.)
  - Neither the 20-row nor the 40-cell list is at least 10 times the sample size (20 < 50; 40 < 50), so the hypergeometric figures are the correct ones for the hands-on. The binomial 41% is correct for large sheets, or when 1-in-10 is read as a per-cell error rate.
- What a clean sample proves: with 0 errors in 5 binomial checks, the exact one-sided 95% upper bound on the error rate is 1 − 0.05^(1/5) = 45.1%. (Computed.) Shukla gives the large-sample rule of thumb: after 30 clean cells, "the true error rate could still be as high as about 3 divided by 30. That is 10%." [source: https://echonerve.substack.com/p/how-to-check-3000-ai-filled-spreadsheet, accessed 2026-11-25]
- Sample size needed for a 95% chance of catching at least one error when p = 0.1: n ≥ ln(0.05)/ln(0.9) = 28.4, so 29 cells. (Computed from the binomial formula.)

## 4. Limits

**Failure mode A: the source does not support the value.** When it happens: any grounded answer. How often and how badly: in Liu, Zhang and Liang's audit of four generative search engines, "only 74.5% of citations support their associated sentence" and "a mere 51.5% of generated sentences are fully supported by citations" [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848, accessed 2026-11-25]. These figures are from 2023 and from other products, not Gemini in Sheets; no Sheets-specific rate was found. Dixon documented a related failure in the Gemini app: inline source labels with no recoverable URL. He concluded the evidence "shows repeated opaque, wrong-remit provenance, not exact pages I opened" [source: https://dixon.ai/posts/ai-cites-wrong-source/, accessed 2026-11-25].

**Failure mode B: the source is out of date (or the cell is).** When it happens: values that change over time, such as headcount, and any sheet reused after generation. Google states that AI function output is inserted as an edit and, when the prompt joins several cells together, "you don't get a prompt to refresh the data" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25]. McKelvey's advice: "Add a 'last generated' date column so stale answers are easy to spot." [source: https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/gemini-in-google-sheets, accessed 2026-11-25]. Dixon recorded the same problem on the reading side in another assistant: Perplexity gave an out-of-date childcare figure while citing pages that contradicted it, and "The links were real. The model read them as if the calendar hadn't moved." [source: https://dixon.ai/posts/ai-cites-wrong-source/, accessed 2026-11-25]. No frequency figure for Sheets was found.

**Failure mode C: credible sources disagree.** No retrieved source documents a Sheets cell whose cited sources conflicted. The nearest evidence comes from two places. Dixon observed that the same question on the same day gave "the wrong answer once and the right one the next, in the identical confident voice both times" [source: https://dixon.ai/posts/ai-cites-wrong-source/, accessed 2026-11-25]. And Google's own two Sheets pages disagree on 350 versus 3,000 cells, which shows official sources can conflict [source: https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/gemini-in-google-sheets, accessed 2026-11-25]. This failure mode is plausible but has no evidence for Sheets; see Appendix B.

**Technical limitation 1: web only, text only, no nesting.** Sources "are available in Google Sheets on the web" [MONITOR] [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25].\[1\] Google's limitations list includes: "Responses are limited to text"; "Embedded AI functions aren't supported"; "You can't undo or redo your function" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].

**Technical limitation 2: quotas and batch size.** "The AI function has short term and long term generation limits. If you reach a long term limit, you temporarily can't click 'Generate.' You can wait for 24 hours and then try again." [MONITOR] [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].\[8\] Google's admin documentation lists the AI function at "No access | 5,000 per month | 25,000 per month" across its business tiers and says "AI function limits are per-cell generation limits" [MONITOR] [source: https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/generative-ai/workspace-with-gemini/about-ai-usage-limits?visit_id=639279582526012092-3222650515&rd=1, accessed 2026-11-25].\[18\]\[19\]\[20\]

**Technical limitation 3: limited context and unstable results.** The function "doesn't have access to your entire spreadsheet or other files in your Google Drive" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].\[8\] Bradshaw tested the function before web grounding existed. He found that other cells in the same row affected results: "the AI function is influenced by data in other cells in the same row, even if the formula doesn't include them." He also found that results tended "to disappear when changes are made to the spreadsheet" [source: https://onlinejournalismblog.com/2025/04/10/google-sheets-has-a-new-ai-function-how-does-it-perform-on-classification-tasks/, accessed 2026-11-25]. No retrieved source says whether this still happens after the October 2026 upgrade promising "improved cell-over-cell consistency" [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25].

**Non-technical risk: false assurance.** Liu et al. found "citation precision is inversely correlated with perceived utility": "the responses that seem more helpful are often those with more unsupported statements or inaccurate citations" [source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09848v1, accessed 2026-11-25].\[21\] Shukla: "A citation per cell makes a bulk fill feel verified, and the feeling is cheap to get." [source: https://echonerve.substack.com/p/how-to-check-3000-ai-filled-spreadsheet, accessed 2026-11-25]. Dixon: "Every non-passing source answer on the board: 0 hedges." [source: https://dixon.ai/posts/ai-cites-wrong-source/, accessed 2026-11-25].\[14\]

**What it is explicitly not for.**
- Google: "Don't rely on Gemini features as medical, legal, financial or other professional advice." [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25]\[8\]
- Not available on Business Starter: McKelvey reports Google's pricing page "marks Gemini in Sheets as not included on Business Starter" [MONITOR] [source: https://justinmckelvey.com/blog/gemini-in-google-sheets, accessed 2026-11-25].\[13\]
- Not for sheets opened through third-party storage: "You can't generate content with the AI function if you access Sheets with other cloud storage providers like Box, Dropbox, or Egnyte" [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15877199?hl=en, accessed 2026-11-25].\[8\]
- Shukla's tiering for decision cells: "open every source. If that is too many cells, the sheet is too big for that use." [source: https://echonerve.substack.com/p/how-to-check-3000-ai-filled-spreadsheet, accessed 2026-11-25]\[2\]

## Appendix B. Unverified claims

**Hook: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.**
- Supported: Google added cell-level web citations to Fill with Gemini / the AI function in Sheets; each AI-generated cell shows the web sources behind it; Google presents this as enabling audit and verification [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/fill-with-gemini-now-provides-cell-level-citations-for-web-sources-and-supports-larger-requests.html, accessed 2026-11-25].
- Unsupported as worded: "On 30 October 2026." The announcement is dated 28 October 2026 and rollout began 20 October 2026; 30 October is the weekly recap [source: https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/weekly-recap-10-30-2026.html?m=1, accessed 2026-11-25]. Recommended wording: "In late October 2026 (announced 28 October)."
- Partially unsupported: "none showed where it came from." For general users, neither the April 2026 nor the October 2025 Google post mentions sources, which is consistent with the hook. However, an undated Google help snippet documents a per-cell Sources view for "Workspace Experiments and Beta programs" users before the general rollout [source: https://support.google.com/docs/answer/15820999?p=ai-function, accessed 2026-11-25].
- Unsupported: "every value looked equally confident." No retrieved source documents whether Sheets displays confidence. Dixon's finding of "0 hedges" on wrong answers comes from the Gemini app, not Sheets [source: https://dixon.ai/posts/ai-cites-wrong-source/, accessed 2026-11-25].
- Unsupported: "you either checked every cell or trusted none." This is framing, not a sourced fact.
- Supported in substance: "a citation shows where a number came from, not whether it's right" matches Shukla's "A source in a cell shows where the AI looked. It does not show that the page says what the cell says." [source: https://echonerve.substack.com/p/how-to-check-3000-ai-filled-spreadsheet, accessed 2026-11-25].\[2\]

**Value proposition: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.**
- 41% figure: SUPPORTED. 1 − 0.9⁵ = 0.40951 under the binomial model [source: https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda366i.htm, accessed 2026-11-25]. For the hands-on's short list, the exact hypergeometric figures are 44.7% (20 cells, 2 wrong) and 42.7% (40 cells, 4 wrong) [source: https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/glossary.htm, accessed 2026-11-25]. The script should say "about 41% for a large sheet; about 43–45% on our 20-company list."
- "Four-step trust ladder" (read, spot-check, cross-check, sign off): UNSUPPORTED as an established framework; no retrieved source names it. The closest sourced practices are Dixon's "require the exact page, then find the claim on it" [source: https://dixon.ai/posts/ai-cites-wrong-source/, accessed 2026-11-25] and Shukla's three-tier checking by how a column is used [source: https://echonerve.substack.com/p/how-to-check-3000-ai-filled-spreadsheet, accessed 2026-11-25]. Present the ladder as the module's own framework.
- Three failure modes: (1) source doesn't support the value: SUPPORTED by Liu et al. for generative search engines generally [source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848, accessed 2026-11-25]; (2) source out of date: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (Google's documentation that outputs don't refresh automatically; Dixon's out-of-date-figure case in another assistant); (3) credible sources disagree: UNSUPPORTED for Sheets; no retrieved case.
- Hands-on (Gemini fills 20 companies with headquarters and headcount; learners audit five cited cells in a shared saved copy): UNVERIFIED. No retrieved source shows the Sources panel persisting in a shared copy, after paste-as-values, or for viewers without an eligible plan. Bradshaw reports results "tend to disappear" on sort, filter or rename [source: https://onlinejournalismblog.com/2025/04/10/google-sheets-has-a-new-ai-function-how-does-it-perform-on-classification-tasks/, accessed 2026-11-25]. Test this in production before recording.
- "Five clean cells prove less than they seem": SUPPORTED by computation; 5 clean cells out of 5 still leave a 95% upper bound of 45.1% on the error rate.
- "A sampling rule your team can adopt": no retrieved source endorses a specific rule. Shukla presents his 30-cell rule as "a number you can afford" [source: https://echonerve.substack.com/p/how-to-check-3000-ai-filled-spreadsheet, accessed 2026-11-25].

**Other framing claims.** "Fill with Gemini previously showed no sources": see the hook above. Headcount accuracy: no retrieved test of headcount fills. The only retrieved company test was a vendor's five-company headquarters check, which was excluded as vendor marketing without deployment evidence.
